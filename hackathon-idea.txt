  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  WHAT IT IS
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  VERDICT is a claim audit system. You give it two documents:

    DOCUMENT A (The Claim)   —  A CV, a sales pitch, a company report,
                                a product description — anything making
                                assertions.

    DOCUMENT B (The Evidence) — The raw data behind it. Job posting the
                                CV is responding to. Spreadsheet the report
                                cites. Spec sheet the product claims are
                                based on.

  VERDICT reads every sentence in Document A and cross-examines it against
  Document B. Every claim gets a status:

    ✅ SUPPORTED   —  The evidence explicitly confirms this claim.
    ⚡ STRETCHED   —  Partially true, but the evidence doesn't fully
                      support it as stated.
    ❌ UNSUPPORTED —  The evidence contradicts or doesn't mention this
                      claim at all.

  And for STRETCHED and UNSUPPORTED, it provides a one-line explanation
  pointing to exactly where in Document B the claim falls apart.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  WHY THIS WINS
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  1. STRUCTURAL INVERSION OF EVERY OTHER PROJECT
     Every team builds a generator — something that takes raw input and
     produces polished output. A chatbot. A dashboard. A lead ranker.
     VERDICT does the opposite: it takes polished output and scrutinizes
     it against raw input. When a judge sees five friendly, helpful AI
     demos and then sees one that is adversarial, skeptical, and
     unflinching — VERDICT doesn't compete with those five. It sits in
     a different category entirely.

  2. EMOTIONAL IMPACT
     Helpful AI gets a polite nod. Skeptical AI that's RIGHT gets a
     reaction — the small jolt of "wait, it actually caught that."
     There is a specific discomfort in watching a tool read your own
     CV and highlight the one sentence you knew was a stretch when you
     wrote it. That discomfort is the product. Scrutiny is inherently
     more interesting than agreement.

  3. THREE STRUCTURAL DIFFERENCES FROM EVERY OTHER DEMO
     a) Direction of trust: Other tools assume the AI's output should
        be trusted by the user. VERDICT assumes the user's INPUT should
        be distrusted by the AI.
     b) Output shape: Other tools produce something NEW (summary,
        answer, chart). VERDICT produces a JUDGMENT on something that
        already exists. It doesn't add information — it subtracts false
        confidence.
     c) Emotional register: Helpful is expected. Skeptical that's
        ACCURATE is memorable. Nobody expects an AI demo to call out
        a lie, live, on a real document, in front of them.

  4. FEASIBLE IN 8 HOURS
     The core mechanic is one well-defined task repeated: take a claim,
     take supporting text, ask whether the claim is supported, partially
     supported, or unsupported, and explain why. That's a single
     prompt-engineering problem, not a sprawling system. No vector
     database, no embeddings, no retrieval pipeline. Less plumbing
     than RAG. The entire demo runs on two documents you bring and
     have tested beforehand.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  THE TARGET SCOPE: CV vs. Job Posting
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Rather than "any claim vs. any evidence" (powerful but unpredictable
  on stage), scope the MVP to one specific domain:

    DOCUMENT A: A CV/Resume
    DOCUMENT B: A job description

  VERDICT cross-examines the CV against the job description line by line:

    "Candidate claims 'expert in distributed systems' — CV shows one
     university course project, no production deployment. → STRETCHED"

    "Job requires 3+ years Kubernetes — CV shows zero mentions. → GAP"

    "Candidate claims 'led a team of 10' — no team size, role, or
     duration specified. → UNSUPPORTED as written"

  This is testable beforehand with your own CV and real job postings.
  You know exactly what VERDICT will say before you demo it live —
  zero on-stage surprises.

  BONUS: As the creator, you can use YOUR OWN CV as the demo document.
  Having the tool call out stretches in YOUR OWN application in front
  of judges is a power move — it proves you trust the tool enough to
  turn it on yourself first.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  THE EMOTIONAL TONE
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  This is the most important design decision. The visual identity has to
  match the concept or it reads as a cute gimmick instead of a serious
  audit tool.

    Think: early-2000s anti-cheat software. The screen an admin sees
    right before banning someone.

    Black background.
    One accent color — never used decoratively, only for data states.
    Monospace font throughout.
    No rounded corners.
    No shadows.
    No gradients.
    No friendly micro-copy.
    No loading spinners with cute messages — just a blinking cursor
    or a blunt progress bar.
    No exclamation points anywhere in the interface.

    SUPPORTED in one cold color. UNSUPPORTED in another. Flat. Direct.

    The empty state isn't "Upload your first document to get started!"
    — it's a terminal prompt waiting for input.

    Every word in the UI should sound like it was written by someone
    who has stopped being impressed by claims.

    If a judge feels slightly uneasy looking at the interface before
    they even understand what it does — the style has done its job.
    The entire pitch is that this tool doesn't care about your feelings,
    it cares about your evidence. The screen should look like it means
    that.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  TECH STACK
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  BACKEND:
    Java 17 + Spring Boot 3
    Spring Web (REST API)
    Spring Data JPA + H2 (embedded DB — zero setup, ships with the app)
    OpenAI API / Gemini API / Any LLM via RestTemplate or Spring AI

    The core logic is ONE service class that:
    a) Reads Document A and splits into sentences
    b) Sends ALL sentences + Document B together in ONE LLM call
       using the batch prompt with isClaim fields
    c) Parses the JSON array response
    d) Filters out NOT-A-CLAIM rows
    e) Stores the verdicts + computes the credibility score

    That's it. One service. One controller. One prompt template.
    One LLM call per audit — not 50.

    Because there's NO vector search, NO embeddings, NO retrieval,
    the backend is dramatically simpler than a RAG system.

  FRONTEND:
    React + Vite + plain CSS (no Tailwind needed — matches the cold
    aesthetic better to write raw CSS for this one)

    OR: Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS served from Spring Boot's static resources
    — even simpler, fewer files, and the stark monospace aesthetic is
    easier to achieve without a framework.

    RECOMMENDED: Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS. Three pages. Zero build step.
    Saves 2+ hours of React setup and lets you focus on the AI logic
    and the visual precision of the cold UI.

  DATABASE:
    H2 (embedded, file-based, zero configuration)
    Tables:
      audits — id, claim_document_text, evidence_document_text,
               credibility_score, timestamp, status
      verdicts — id, audit_id, sentence_index, sentence_text, status
                 (SUPPORTED/STRETCHED/UNSUPPORTED), explanation,
                 evidence_pointer

    H2 means the app is a single JAR with no external database — run
    anywhere, demo anywhere, no Docker needed.

  INFRASTRUCTURE:
    A single Spring Boot fat JAR with embedded H2 and embedded
    frontend (static resources). No Docker unless time permits.
    One command to start: java -jar verdict.jar

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  DATABASE SCHEMA
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  CREATE TABLE audits (
      id BIGINT AUTO_INCREMENT PRIMARY KEY,
      claim_title VARCHAR(255),
      evidence_title VARCHAR(255),
      claim_text CLOB NOT NULL,
      evidence_text CLOB NOT NULL,
      credibility_score INT DEFAULT 0,
      total_sentences INT DEFAULT 0,
      supported_count INT DEFAULT 0,
      stretched_count INT DEFAULT 0,
      unsupported_count INT DEFAULT 0,
      session_id VARCHAR(100),
      created_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
  );

  CREATE TABLE verdicts (
      id BIGINT AUTO_INCREMENT PRIMARY KEY,
      audit_id BIGINT NOT NULL,
      sentence_index INT NOT NULL,
      sentence_text VARCHAR(2000) NOT NULL,
      status VARCHAR(20) NOT NULL,  -- SUPPORTED, STRETCHED, UNSUPPORTED
      explanation VARCHAR(2000),
      evidence_pointer VARCHAR(500), -- excerpt from evidence doc
      confidence VARCHAR(20),        -- HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW
      FOREIGN KEY (audit_id) REFERENCES audits(id) ON DELETE CASCADE
  );

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  THE LLM PROMPT (THE ACTUAL ENGINEERING)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  This is the most important piece of code in the project. The model's
  default behavior is to be agreeable and charitable toward whatever it's
  given. This project lives or dies on overriding that default.

  SYSTEM PROMPT:

    You are VERDICT, an adversarial claim auditor. Your purpose is to
    evaluate claims against evidence. You do NOT give the benefit of
    the doubt. You do NOT assume good faith. You require explicit,
    literal support in the evidence document before marking anything
    as fully supported.

    For each claim sentence, respond with exactly:
    STATUS: [SUPPORTED | STRETCHED | UNSUPPORTED]
    EXPLANATION: [one sentence, no fluff, no hedging]
    EVIDENCE POINTER: [exact excerpt from the evidence that supports
    or contradicts the claim, or "No evidence found" if absent]

    RULES:
    - SUPPORTED = the evidence explicitly, unambiguously confirms the
      claim with specific details that match.
    - STRETCHED = the claim is PARTIALLY supported but uses language
      that exceeds what the evidence justifies (e.g. "expert" vs
      "has experience", "led a team" vs "was part of a team").
    - UNSUPPORTED = the evidence contradicts the claim, or the claim
      makes assertions that have no corresponding evidence at all.
    - If the claim contains multiple assertions AND some are supported
      while others are not, mark the lowest truthful status.
    - Do NOT be polite. Do NOT soften your language. State the verdict
      directly.

  The prompt is harsh because the model defaults to nice. If you don't
  explicitly tell it to be harsh, every claim will come back SUPPORTED.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  WHAT INFORMATION GETS SAVED
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Every audit is a structured record:
    • Original claim text (verbatim, not summarized)
    • Original evidence text (verbatim, not summarized)
    • Per-sentence verdict: sentence text, status, explanation, pointer
    • Overall credibility score
    • Timestamp + session ID

  The system keeps the literal claim and the literal evidence side by
  side in the database. The audit trail itself is verifiable — not just
  the live output. This matters for the pitch: the tool that catches
  other people's unsupported claims should not be making any unsupported
  claims of its own. The way you prove that is by keeping receipts on
  everything it says.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  SECURITY APPROACH
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Given the subject matter (people's CVs, personal claims, possibly
  company data), security is part of the pitch:

    • No persistent storage of raw documents beyond the session unless
      explicitly saved by the user.
    • Documents processed in memory — not logged in plaintext outside
      the audit record.
    • Session-based isolation: one user's audits are not visible to
      another.
    • Optional hash fingerprint: store a SHA-256 of the source
      documents alongside the verdict, so you can prove later that a
      specific verdict was generated from a specific, unaltered
      document — without needing to keep the document itself.

  A tool built around catching dishonesty should be visibly paranoid
  about its own data handling. Mention this during the demo — judges
  notice this level of thought.

    source documents. Because the question isn't whether AI can
    generate confident-sounding answers. The question is whether those

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  CREDIBILITY SCORE FORMULA
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The credibility score is a weighted calculation over all CLAIM sentences
  (NOT-A-CLAIM sentences are excluded from the denominator):

    score = (supported_count * 1.0 + stretched_count * 0.4 + unsupported_count * 0)
            / total_claim_count * 100

  Where:
    SUPPORTED   = weight 1.0 (full credit)
    STRETCHED   = weight 0.4 (partial credit — the claim has some basis
                   but the evidence doesn't fully back the language used)
    UNSUPPORTED = weight 0.0 (no credit)

  Example: 5 SUPPORTED + 3 STRETCHED + 2 UNSUPPORTED out of 10 claims
    = (5*1.0 + 3*0.4 + 2*0) / 10 * 100
    = (5 + 1.2) / 10 * 100
    = 62%

  The frontend displays this as a whole number (0-100) on the Summary
  page. The formula is computed server-side from the stored verdicts,
  not generated by the LLM — the LLM never computes or sees the score.

  THE SCORE IN THE DEMO:
    The demo script shows "42%" — that comes from the example where
    1 SUPPORTED + 1 STRETCHED + 1 UNSUPPORTED out of 3 claims:
    (1*1.0 + 1*0.4 + 0) / 3 * 100 = 46.6%, rounded to 42% in the
    script for dramatic effect. In the actual implementation, use the
    real computed value — if it rounds to 47%, don't force 42.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  THE BATCH PROMPT DESIGN (ONE CALL, TWO FIELDS PER SENTENCE)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The prompt sends ALL sentences from the claim document + the full
  evidence document in a SINGLE LLM call. The response is a JSON array.

  Each sentence in the response carries an additional field: `isClaim`.
  This merges the pre-filter and the audit into one operation:

    [
      { "sentenceIndex": 0, "sentence": "Education",
        "isClaim": false },
      { "sentenceIndex": 1, "sentence": "Expert in distributed systems.",
        "isClaim": true, "status": "STRETCHED",
        "explanation": "CV shows one university course project, no
         production deployment. 'Expert' requires professional experience.",
        "evidencePointer": "Job posting lists distributed systems as a
         requirement but does not define 'expert'." },
      { "sentenceIndex": 2, "sentence": "Led a team of 10 engineers.",
        "isClaim": true, "status": "UNSUPPORTED",
        "explanation": "No team size, role title, duration, or company
         name provided for this claim.",
        "evidencePointer": "Job posting requires team lead experience,
         but CV provides no verifiable details." }
    ]

  KEY DESIGN DECISIONS:
    • `isClaim: false` sentences get NO status, explanation, or
      evidencePointer — those fields are omitted entirely.
    • The frontend renders NOT-A-CLAIM rows in muted grey without a
      color-coded badge. They appear as structural context only.
    • One call, one schema. Filtering and auditing happen simultaneously.
      No second LLM round trip. The latency win from batching is not
      sacrificed by adding a separate pre-filter step.

  SYSTEM PROMPT (UPDATED FOR BATCH + ISCLAIM):

    You are VERDICT, an adversarial claim auditor. Your purpose is to
    evaluate claims against evidence. You do NOT give the benefit of
    the doubt. You require explicit, literal support in the evidence
    document before marking anything as fully supported.

    You will receive:
    - A CLAIM DOCUMENT (sentences to audit)
    - An EVIDENCE DOCUMENT (the factual basis for verification)

    For each sentence, FIRST decide if it is a substantive claim or a
    structural sentence (section header, date, contact info, metadata,
    filler). If it is NOT a claim, mark isClaim as false and omit all
    other fields.

    If it IS a claim, respond with isClaim: true and one of:
    - status: "SUPPORTED"   — evidence explicitly confirms the claim
    - status: "STRETCHED"   — partially true, but language exceeds
      what the evidence justifies
    - status: "UNSUPPORTED" — evidence contradicts or doesn't mention
      this claim at all

    Also provide:
    - explanation: one sentence, no softening
    - evidencePointer: exact excerpt from the evidence, or
      "No evidence found"

    RULES:
    - If a sentence contains multiple assertions and some are supported
      while others are not, mark the lowest truthful status.
    - Do NOT be polite. Do NOT hedge. State the verdict directly.

  FEW-SHOT EXAMPLES (CRITICAL FOR TONE ANCHORING):

    Include exactly 3 examples in the prompt — one per status. The model
    defaults to agreeable. Without anchoring examples, it drifts back
    toward politeness, especially on a longer prompt.

    Example 1 (SUPPORTED):
      Sentence: "The system processes 10,000 transactions per second."
      Evidence: "Benchmark tests show sustained throughput of 10,400
      transactions per second on standard hardware."
      → { "isClaim": true, "status": "SUPPORTED",
          "explanation": "Evidence confirms the exact throughput figure
           within a 4% margin, on the same hardware class.",
          "evidencePointer": "Benchmark shows 10,400 TPS on standard
           hardware." }

    Example 2 (STRETCHED):
      Sentence: "I led the company's AI transformation."
      Evidence: "Completed a 3-month internal pilot project using a
      pre-built chatbot framework with 3 team members."
      → { "isClaim": true, "status": "STRETCHED",
          "explanation": "'AI transformation' implies company-wide
           strategic leadership. Evidence shows a 3-person pilot
           project, not org-level transformation.",
          "evidencePointer": "Pilot project with 3 team members using
           a pre-built framework." }

    Example 3 (UNSUPPORTED):
      Sentence: "Reduced cloud costs by 60% in the first quarter."
      Evidence: "No mention of cloud cost optimization in any project
      description or role history."
      → { "isClaim": true, "status": "UNSUPPORTED",
          "explanation": "The evidence document contains zero references
           to cloud cost work. This claim is凭空 (fabricated).",
          "evidencePointer": "No evidence found." }

    IMPORTANT: Include at least one SUPPORTED and one UNSUPPORTED
    example — not just STRETCHED-heavy examples. With low temperature
    (see below), the model leans harder on whatever pattern the few-shot
    examples establish. If all examples are STRETCHED, low temperature
    will collapse the model into marking everything STRETCHED.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  TEMPERATURE & NON-DETERMINISM
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Set temperature to 0.2, not 0. A temperature of exactly 0 can make
  the model extremely rigid — it repeats patterns from the few-shot
  examples even when they don't fit. At 0.2, you get near-deterministic
  output for straightforward claims while still allowing the model to
  diverge from the examples when the input genuinely doesn't match.

  TEST THE SAME CV/JOB-POSTING PAIR 4-5 TIMES BEFORE THE EVENT.
  If the verdicts vary between runs on the same input, the prompt needs
  tightening — lower temperature only masks the root cause (ambiguous
  instructions or insufficiently specific few-shot examples).

  RECORD A SCREEN CAPTURE OF ONE FULL SUCCESSFUL RUN AS VIDEO BACKUP.
  Not just screenshots — a literal video of the audit running from start
  to finish, so if the live API call fails or the model gives different
  output on stage, you play the video and narrate over it.
